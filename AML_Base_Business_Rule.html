01 What this base layer does

Turns claims no one event can explain on its own into a shape a brand team can read: which patients are on treatment, what they're taking, how long, and which line of therapy that represents right now.

Every AML patient's record is a scatter of individual transactions — a pharmacy claim here, a medical infusion code there, a diagnosis on a hospital bill. None of those rows says "this patient just moved to their second line of therapy." This base layer is the set of rules that gets from the scattered rows to that sentence, reproducibly, for every patient in the data, every refresh.

Where the data comes from

Three claim families feed the pipeline, each standardized to its own national format before anything else happens:

Rx — pharmacy claims (NCPDP)
Retail and mail order. Carries its own days-supply on the claim, so DOS almost always comes straight from the data rather than a lookup.
Mx — medical claims (CMS-1500)
Diagnosis codes (ICD-9/10), procedure codes (CPT/HCPCS), infusions, stem cell transplant and bone marrow biopsy procedures. Carries no days-supply — DOS is assigned per product instead.

A third family, Hx (hospital claims, UB-92/UB-04), contributes context elsewhere in the broader oncology pipeline but isn't a direct input to episode or regimen construction here.

Scope noteThis page documents the AML base layer specifically — the DOS/Grace values, backbone hierarchies, and eligibility logic below are AML's own, not shared defaults reused from another tumor type. Two sibling base layers exist for other brands/indications and are documented separately.

04 Which drugs are in scope

28 products, but patients don't all compete on the same ladder. Which hierarchy applies depends on a separate judgment call — patient intensity — made once per patient.

When more than one drug is active at once, the backbone is whichever one ranks highest on a fixed priority list, and AML runs two different lists depending on whether the patient is intensive-chemo eligible (IC-ELIG) or ineligible (IC-INELIG). The same drug can sit at a different rank — or be absent entirely — depending on which list applies.

How a claim becomes a product

Every claim resolves to one of 28 FINAL_PRODUCT_NAME values before anything downstream runs. One product gets special handling: Filgrastim is a supportive-care product, excluded entirely before DOS/Grace assignment — it can never create or extend an episode, regimen, or line of therapy.

Cytarabine specifically does not resolve to one product

It splits into three by dose intensity before anything else happens — see Section 09 for the full split.

05 How long one claim covers a patient

Two numbers per product: Days of Supply (DOS), how long a single fill is assumed to last, and Grace, how much extra time beyond that is still called "still on treatment." Together they decide whether the next claim joins the same episode or starts a new one.

A claim's coverage window is [claim date, claim date + DOS + Grace]. The next claim for the same product, same patient joins the same episode if its date falls inside that window — otherwise a new episode starts. Section 06 covers what happens next.

DOS: where the number comes from

Rx claims
The claim's own PRODUCT_DAYS_SUPPLY field, used as-is. If it's missing or non-positive, the pipeline falls back to a flat 28 days — a blunt, file-wide default, not a per-product judgment.
Mx (PX) claims
No days-supply field exists on a medical claim at all, so a fixed per-product value stands in instead — anywhere from 1 day (most IV/infusion products) to 28 days (cycle-based orals dosed as PX). The full list is in the table below.

Grace: the same idea, as a separate number

Grace is not derived from DOS — it's its own per-product value, assigned independently. A product nobody has reviewed gets NULL for both DOS and Grace, never a default, so it can't silently create or extend a line of therapy.

06 Building regimens

Three mechanical steps turn claims into regimens: Start-of-Bucket, Episode, Regimen. None of the three makes a clinical judgment — that's reserved for line-of-therapy assignment in Section 07.

07 When a change is a new line

Two rounds, run in order, for every regimen after the first. One gap test, then — only if the gap passes — a backbone-change test with one named exception.

Round 1 — the gap test

Count the days between the previous regimen's end date and this regimen's start date. More than 90 days, and it's automatically a new line — the backbone doesn't even get checked.

Round 2 — the backbone test (only reached if Round 1 passes)

The named exceptionVenclexta ↔ HMA switches, in either direction, do not count as a backbone change — even though Venclexta and HMA are different backbones on both hierarchies. Since Inqovi and Onureg now resolve to the generic HMA backbone (Section 04), this exception covers Venclexta switching with Azacitidine, Decitabine, Inqovi, or Onureg — any of the four reads as "HMA" to this rule.

Why the exception exists

Adding an HMA alongside Venclexta, or dropping Venclexta down to HMA maintenance, is a dose adjustment within the same clinical intent — not a switch to a new regimen family. Counting it as a new line would manufacture line-of-therapy churn out of what's clinically a continuation.

08 Patient intensity, and a rule in transition

Which backbone hierarchy applies to a patient depends on one classification, made once: intensive-chemo eligible (IC-ELIG) or ineligible (IC-INELIG). Two different versions of that classification exist in the current codebase side by side.

This classification is made once per patient, for their whole journey — not re-evaluated per line. If a patient has taken even one IC-ELIG-only product at any point in their journey, they're classified IC-ELIG for the whole journey — even in a line where they only take IC-INELIG-hierarchy products.

Rule A — product-only (current production)

Look at every product the patient has ever taken. If any of them is restricted to the IC-ELIG list — an intensive chemo backbone like S-DAC/HI-DAC, Vanflyta, Vyxeos, or one of the cytotoxic combination agents — the patient is IC-ELIG for their entire journey. Otherwise, IC-INELIG.

What changes if Rule B shipsThe two rules can disagree on the same patient. A 70-year-old on Tibsovo and Idhifa alone is IC-INELIG under both rules — but a 55-year-old on the same two drugs is IC-INELIG under Rule A (neither drug is IC-ELIG-only) and IC-ELIG under Rule B (age ≤65 with no forced-inelig override). Any trend line spanning the cutover date needs to say which rule produced which side of it.

09 Cytarabine's three-way split

One generic drug, three products, by dose. The split happens before DOS and Grace are assigned — cytarabine never exists as a single undifferentiated product downstream of it.

Why the split existsDose intensity is clinically decisive for cytarabine in a way it isn't for most other AML products — low-dose cytarabine is an IC-INELIG backbone in its own right, while standard- and high-dose cytarabine only ever appear on the IC-ELIG hierarchy, bucketed together as S-DAC/HI-DAC. Treating all three as one product would blur a distinction that decides which hierarchy, and which backbone, the patient's line of therapy resolves to.

10 Eligibility flags

Five flags decide which patients a given KPI is even allowed to count. None of them touch the patient pool itself — they're a second gate, applied per-analysis, on top of population membership.

Every flag asks a version of the same question: has this patient been observably active — a diagnosis, a medical claim, or a pharmacy claim — in every semester of a defined lookback window? A gap semester with zero activity fails the flag, even if the patient has activity before and after it.

Anchored to AML treatment start
DX_LB_ELIG_FLG — active (Mx or Rx) in both the 6-month and 12-month windows before first diagnosis.

TX_TXL_MX_ELIG_FLG — continuously active on medical claims across the patient's full AML treatment span, semester by semester.

TX_TXL_RX_ELIG_FLG — the same continuity check, on pharmacy claims.
Anchored to Venclexta start specifically
TX_MX_LB_ELIG_VEN_FLG / TX_RX_LB_ELIG_VEN_FLG — lookback-window activity before the patient's first Venclexta claim, medical and pharmacy respectively.
Two VEN-specific flags exist because Venclexta's own journey can start well after a patient's broader AML treatment didA patient eligible by the AML-anchored flags can still fail the Venclexta-anchored ones, if their activity history doesn't hold up around whenever they specifically started Venclexta. Pick the anchor that matches what the KPI is actually measuring.
